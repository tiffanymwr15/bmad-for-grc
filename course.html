BMAD is the Breakthrough Method for Agile AI-Driven Development. It is an open-source way to build software with AI agents, by writing the important decisions down before any code exists.

What BMAD is

Most coding agents are handed a goal and asked to implement it. BMAD puts a method in front of that. The work covers the whole effort: what to build, how it holds together, and how it changes as you learn.

Specialized perspectives weigh in, in order. Product clarifies what is in scope. Architecture decides how it holds together. The person who will use the result shapes what they need to see. Development builds one piece at a time. Testing checks that the piece does what the decision said. Each of those leaves a document. The next step loads that document instead of guessing.

A later part of the method, the BMad Loop, can take the resulting stories and run the build without someone pasting the next command. It implements a story, checks that the tests pass, reviews the change, commits it, and moves on. When it cannot decide, it stops and asks.

BMAD
Breakthrough Method for Agile AI-Driven Development. Decisions stay explicit, context carries forward, and the amount of planning matches the size of the change.
BMad Loop
The unattended build cycle. A queue of stories goes in. Reviewed commits come out. A decision the loop cannot make becomes a stop.
The point
An agent implements whatever you left unstated. BMAD is the practice of not leaving the important parts unstated.

Why it matters for GRC engineering

GRC engineering turns an obligation into something a system can enforce and an assessor can trace. The pieces are ordinary: a named requirement, a check that can fail, a record of the result, and a catalog entry that uses the same words as the check.

That work is now often done with a coding agent. The agent is good at the check. It is careless about the decision the check depends on. Ask it to “add the control” and it will also invent which requirement applies, what a failure looks like, what gets stored as evidence, and who may accept an exception.

In a product feature, that guess becomes rework. In GRC engineering, the guess becomes proof. Someone later treats the green run as evidence that the requirement is met. If the requirement was never named, the evidence does not hold.

BMAD matters here because it keeps that decision in front of the code. You name the obligation, the fail case, the evidence, and the owner while they are still cheap to change. The agent builds against those words. When the loop stops, the question goes to the person who owns it: the control owner, the person who reads the evidence, or security. It does not get answered by whoever happens to be at the keyboard.

This course is that translation. It is not tied to one framework, one pipeline, or one tool. The worked example is a privileged-access review, because the shape is easy to replay on any control you actually operate.

Lesson 1

control does not

BMAD matters because a coding agent will implement whatever decision you left unstated. In GRC engineering, that fill-in becomes a control you cannot defend.

The trade

Spend the tokens before the code. Make the obligation, the fail case, the evidence, and the owner explicit while they are still cheap to change. Once a run is green and someone treats the record as proof, the same decision is expensive.

The method’s order is clarify, plan, build and verify, then learn. Product, architecture, the evidence consumer, development, and testing weigh in before implementation starts.

Two familiar failures

In the policy only

The requirement is real. Nothing checks it, and nothing records when it holds or fails.

In the system only

A check passes. Nobody wrote the obligation, the owner, the false pass, or where the evidence goes.

Explicit means five answers

A later reader, human or agent, can answer these without guessing. A blank is the defect.

  1. 1
    ObligationWhich requirement, which framework, which system.
  2. 2
    IntentWhat must pass, and what must fail.
  3. 3
    EvidenceWhat record proves it, where it lives, who can read it.
  4. 4
    OwnerWho accepts a scope change, an exception, or a residual risk.
  5. 5
    BoundaryWhat the agent may not do to get a green run. Invent an ID, weaken a check, log a secret, widen scope.

Autonomy is the loop taking the next story without you pasting a command. Reach is the stop arriving at the person who owns it. Fewer questions is not the goal. Unowned answers are.

Check yourself

1 Someone says “add encryption at rest and make the check pass.” Which of the five answers are still missing?

Usually all five. The phrase names neither the requirement nor the system. It does not say what must fail. Evidence, owner, and the forbidden list are unstated, so the agent will fill them in.

2 Why is a wrong decision cheaper in a planning note than in a green run?

In the note, the next phase simply loads a different decision. In a green run, that decision is already evidence and history someone may rely on.

3 The loop stops because the check needs a data store the spec never mentioned. Implementation detail, or scope?

Scope. A new store changes what the system reads, what it keeps, and who owns the exposure. Naming a branch would be the implementation-detail version of a stop.

Lesson 2

Decisions travel
as documents,

Each phase writes something the next phase loads. The agent is never asked to invent the previous decision. A small, already-decided change skips ahead to build.

Four phases

Pick a track by risk

A five-line edit that changes what a control proves is not a small change. Prefer this test over a story count.

Who owns the decision

The named agents are perspectives. One person may wear several, as long as the decision is written down. One chat playing all of them, with no record, is the failure.

Obligation
Which requirement, which system, what is out of scope, what “done” means for the person who will rely on the evidence.
Evidence consumer
Whether an assessor can tell pass from fail without reading the implementation.
Architecture
Where the check runs, what it may see, what it emits, and which choice would be expensive to reverse.
Sequencing
Stories small enough to review, ordered so a fail case exists before a green run is trusted.
Implementation
Code and tests that satisfy the story and do not widen it.
Proof
A pass case, a fail case, and the edge that would make a broken control look healthy.

Check yourself

1 The requirement, owner, and evidence location already exist. You are only stopping a false positive. Which track?

Quick Flow. Write a short tech spec that cites the existing decision and names the case that must still fail.

2 A new requirement needs a check, a fail case, a run, and a catalog entry. Why is Quick Flow a poor fit?

The meaning of the control is not in an artifact yet, and the change crosses several pieces. Quick Flow would let the agent choose the interpretation in code. Use the Method track. If you will show the result as assurance, add the enterprise notes.

3 Who owns the evidence layout, the place the check runs, and which requirement is in scope?

The evidence consumer, the architect, and the obligation owner. Those are three decisions, even when one engineer is typing.

Lesson 3 · a general change

Follow one
requirement
all the way
to proof.

Privileged access must be reviewed on a fixed interval. Today that sentence lives in a policy. The work is a check that fails when a review is missing or stale, a record an assessor can read, and a catalog entry in the same words. The stack stays unnamed. Replay this shape on whatever you operate.

In scope

The interval

Privileged accounts already in the system of record. A current review passes. A missing or stale review fails and names the account.

Out of scope

The neighbors

Who counts as privileged. Granting or removing access. Multi-factor authentication. Leave those closed, or the agent will build them.

The path

Select a step. The fail case comes before the check, so a broken control cannot pass by accident.

    Readiness, before you build

    Pass
    Requirement, non-goals, pass case, fail case, evidence fields, and an owner are written down.
    Concerns
    Proceed, and write the loose end into the story. Example: the interval’s timezone is still vague, so the check story must name it.
    Fail
    Stop. No owner for an exception, no fail case, or the word “access” standing in for a named requirement.

    Hand the loop the story queue. Do not hand it the sentence “automate access reviews.” A later one-line fix, once this decision exists, is Quick Flow and cites the requirement it still satisfies.

    Check yourself

    1 What does the agent build if granting and removing access are not marked out of scope?

    Provisioning, removal, and often ticketing. “Automate access reviews” is a wide phrase. The non-goals are what keep this a review-interval claim.

    2 What do you lose by writing the check before the fail case?

    A picture of the miss that does not depend on the code you are about to write. Without it, a check can read the wrong date, or check nothing, and still look finished.

    3 A break-glass account cannot show a review, and nobody owns that exception. Pass, concerns, or fail?

    Fail. An exception with no owner is permission to deviate that nobody holds. A timezone ambiguity is a concern. A missing owner is not.

    Lesson 4

    A stop is
    a decision
    looking for
    its owner.

    The BMad Loop takes a queue of stories, implements each one, requires the tests to pass, reviews, and commits. It will stop. On one machine that stop reaches only the person at the keyboard. The control owner, the assessor, and the security reviewer never see the question that belongs to them.

    Where a stop goes

    Route this stop

    Choose the owner. Implementation detail continues. The other three pause until that owner writes the answer.

    Before the next change

    You would not accept a passing run that cannot name its requirement. Do not accept an agent session that cannot name it either.
